# The Problem

*Last updated: 2026-06-02*

---

## The Core Pain

Every year, thousands of MBA graduates and ambitious professionals leave top programs with two things: a strong business instinct and no idea how to actually start a company.

They know how to analyze markets, structure deals, and present to boards. They don't know how to:
- Set up a legal entity without a lawyer charging €5,000
- Build anything technical without hiring developers they can't afford
- Get their first customer without a brand, a team, or a track record
- Use AI tools beyond ChatGPT for a single prompt

The result: most of them go back to consulting or banking. Not because they lack ambition, but because the operational overhead of starting is invisibly high — and nobody helps them with the boring, blocking mechanics.

---

## What Makes It Worse

**The incubator gap:** Existing incubators want equity, want you to relocate, and want a traction narrative before you have a product. They're built for the 1% who already know how to start, not the 95% who are stuck before they begin.

**The community problem:** MBA networks are enormous and mostly useless for founding. The signal-to-noise is terrible. The people who've actually done it — bootstrapped, gone AI-native, scaled without VC — are not in those groups.

**The AI illiteracy gap:** Most MBAs know AI exists but don't know how to make it work for them. They can't build with it. They don't know how to automate operations with it. The promise of "AI does everything" is real but unreachable without a guide.

**The founder loneliness problem:** Starting alone is brutal. Starting together is hard because trust is hard to build. The infrastructure for co-founder matching and peer accountability is primitive.

---

## Who Feels This Pain Most

**Primary:** MBA students and recent graduates (0–3 years post-MBA) who have:
- A clear ambition to start something
- A business idea in early stage (or several)
- No technical co-founder, no AI skills, no entity, no processes
- Money for tools but not for lawyers, developers, or advisors

**Adjacent:**
- Consultants (McKinsey, Bain, BCG, Big 4) going independent
- Bankers exploring early-stage investing or their own ventures
- Mid-career professionals with industry expertise wanting to productize it

---

## The Real Stakes

This is not a small market. There are ~200,000 MBA graduates per year globally. Top 50 programs produce ~50,000. If even 20% seriously consider founding at some point, that's 10,000 people per year per cohort who need what we're describing.

The cost of doing nothing: talented, ambitious people spend the prime years of their potential building other people's companies. The world loses the companies they could have built.

## Why Students — Motivation & Messaging

### Externe Botschaft (für Partner-Unis, Öffentlichkeit)
Studierende stehen vor einer strukturellen Verschiebung:
- Klassische Einstiegspositionen werden durch AI enger — gute Jobs sind schwieriger zu bekommen
- AI-Kompetenz ist nicht mehr optional, sondern Grundvoraussetzung
- Praktisches Gründungswissen — also wirklich eine Firma gebaut und betrieben zu haben — ist ein Differenzierungsmerkmal, das kein Studium liefert

Für Partner-Unis ist Entropic wie das **Sparkassen Börsenspiel, aber mit echtem Geld und echter Firma**: Studierende lernen Gründen nicht theoretisch, sondern indem sie es tun — mit echter Haftung, echten Kunden, echten Entscheidungen.

### Interne Realität (ehrlich)
Wir wollen auch Geld verdienen. Das ist legitim und kein Widerspruch. Das externe Motiv (Studenten helfen, Gründerkultur stärken) muss jedoch in der Außenkommunikation überwiegen — weil es das ist, was Partner-Unis, Mentoren und Studenten anspricht, und weil es auch wahr ist.
