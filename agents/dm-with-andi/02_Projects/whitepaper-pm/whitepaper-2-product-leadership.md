## Perplexity-Prompt

---

**Research prompt: How Product Leadership Evolves in the Age of AI**

I am writing a white paper on how the role of the product leader (Head of Product, VP Product, CPO) — specifically managers of multiple PMs — is evolving in the context of the Product Operating Model and AI. The ideas below are my initial hypotheses. Please challenge them where evidence or practice contradicts them.

This white paper is the companion to a first paper focused on individual PMs. If WP1 is about what PMs must become, WP2 is about what their leaders must become to enable that.

---

**Core Beliefs**
- The Product Operating Model makes more sense than ever — and product leaders are its guardians.
- Leadership in the age of AI is less about decisions and more about context, principles, and connections.
- The moat is increasingly in distribution — product leaders must bridge product and go-to-market.
- Leading by principles scales; leading by presence does not.
- Psychological safety and mental health are not soft skills — they are structural leadership responsibilities.

---

**1. What stays from the Product Operating Model**
Many leadership practices from POM remain valid and become more important with AI. Analyze which of the following endure — and which become obsolete or transform:

- Empowered teams over feature teams — the product leader as protector of this principle
- Strong leadership ≠ command-and-control: Real leadership is setting direction and guardrails, not dictating every decision. Analyze how the best product leaders operationalize this — and what makes it so hard to sustain under pressure.
- The "flotilla of kayaks" model: teams move in an aligned direction while retaining independent exploration. What does the product leader do to maintain alignment without collapsing autonomy?
- Decision-making framework: decisions should sit with the person closest to the problem, with consultative input from others. How do product leaders build this as a cultural default — not just a stated principle?
- Cascading strategy: teams receive context, not tasks
- Coaching over managing: 1:1s, calibration, development
- Protecting discovery from delivery pressure
- Portfolio prioritization across teams
- Being the cultural carrier of the Product Operating Model

---

**2. Scenarios: How does Product Leadership change?**

- **Scenario A – The Coach-Leader:** Less control, more coaching and context-setting. Applies Liz Wiseman's Multiplier principles — how do the best leaders amplify the intelligence of their teams rather than hoard it? What are the Diminisher traps product leaders fall into?
- **Scenario B – The Culture Builder:** Builds a learning culture, creates psychological safety, leads by principles. How do you build an org that learns fast, experiments often, and processes failure constructively?
- **Scenario C – The Connector:** Creates connections between people (cross-team, cross-functional) and between ideas (pattern recognition, distributing insights). Builds and opens their go-to-market network for their PMs.
- **Scenario D – The Portfolio Owner:** P&L ownership, org design decisions, prioritization across teams. When to split or merge teams, when to add or remove a leadership layer.
- **Scenario E – The AI-Augmented Leader:** Uses AI for calibration, coaching, self-reflection, and strategic analysis. What does the 24/7 AI leadership coach look like?

For each: What triggers it? What does the leader's week look like? What skills are required?

---

**3. Leading by Principles**
When the product leader is not in the room, what guides decisions? Analyze:
- How do the best product leaders write, communicate, and embed decision principles?
- How do principles differ from rules — and why does that matter for autonomous teams?
- Examples from companies where leadership principles visibly shaped product decisions
- How does AI change this — can principles be embedded into AI tooling and coaching?

---

**4. The Moat is in Distribution**
Analyze the hypothesis that competitive advantage increasingly lies in go-to-market, not just product:
- Should product leaders bring their PMs closer to sales and distribution? How?
- What does it mean for a Head of Product to build their own GTM network — and how do they use it to bring market intelligence into the org?
- How do the best product orgs bridge product and sales/marketing today?
- Is this a new skill or a rediscovery of something that was always true?

---

**5. Building a Learning Culture & Creating Connections**
- What specific practices create a learning culture in a product organization?
- How does the product leader become a connector — of people, ideas, and signals?
- How does async communication and written clarity become a leadership tool at scale?
- What is the product leader's role in knowledge distribution vs. knowledge hoarding?
- **Curiosity as a leadership practice** — modeling the behavior of asking *why* before judging *what*; how does this shape team culture and stakeholder trust?
- **"Yes, and" as a cultural norm** — how do the best product leaders create environments where ideas are developed rather than blocked?
- **Relationships = speed** — the counterintuitive truth: teams that invest in relationship work move faster long-term. How do product leaders make space for this — and how do they measure it?
- **The human moat in the AI age:** AI navigates code and content — but competing priorities, stakeholder alignment, and shared understanding remain human work. How do product leaders build these capabilities deliberately? And: which AI tools can actively support stakeholder alignment — and how do you integrate them without replacing the human connection?

---

**6. Psychological Safety & Mental Health as Leadership Responsibilities**
- How does the product leader actively create psychological safety — not as a soft skill, but as a structural practice? How do they respond to failure, dissent, bad news?
- Mental health as an org-level responsibility: workload transparency, expectation management, rest as strategy
- How does the product leader recognize early signs of burnout or demoralization in their PM org?
- What do leading companies do structurally to protect PM wellbeing?
- **Criticize ideas in public, praise people privately — and in public.** The common pattern (praise publicly, criticize privately) sounds safe but creates a culture where ideas are never honestly challenged. The better principle: challenge ideas openly so the whole team learns, but when praising or recognizing people, do both — privately so it feels genuine, and publicly so others see what good looks like. Analyze how this principle shapes psychological safety, learning culture, and team performance.

---

**7. Multiplier Leadership (Liz Wiseman)**
Apply the Multiplier framework to product leadership specifically:
- What does a Multiplier product leader look like vs. a Diminisher?
- Which Diminisher patterns are most common in product orgs — and why?
- How does AI change the dynamic (does it amplify or reduce Diminisher behavior)?
- Concrete practices to shift from Diminisher to Multiplier as a product leader

---

**8. Hiring & Calibrating PMs**
- What does a great PM look like in an AI-augmented team — and how do you recognize it?
- How does the calibration process need to change when PMs have more autonomy and AI handles more execution?
- What interview signals predict PM excellence in 2026?

---

**9. How leading companies are already working this way**
Concrete examples of strong product leadership in practice (Figma, Notion, Linear, Stripe, Airbnb, etc.). Draw from the Lenny Rachitsky podcast, SVPG, and other sources. Reference: https://www.svpg.com/product-coaching-and-ai/

---

**10. Implications for Org Design**
- When do you need a leadership layer between PM and C-Level — and when is it overhead?
- How does AI change span of control for product leaders?
- What does the ideal product org structure look like in 3 years?
- **The end of the classic trio:** PM–Design–Engineering is dissolving and fusing into a new model. The "Product Builder" — a shared foundation across disciplines, with deep expertise in one area — is emerging as the new baseline. How do product leaders hire, develop, and structure teams around this model?
- **AI raises the baseline:** The repetitive, pattern-based ~80% of product work can now be done by anyone with the right tools. What remains are complex trade-offs requiring judgment. How does this change org design, role definitions, and hiring bars?
- Smaller, cross-functional teams become more likely — how do leaders manage coherence and culture at smaller scale?
- **Leaders must create guardrails:** security, QA, and review systems for AI-assisted workflows are a structural leadership responsibility — not a technical detail. How are leading orgs building these?

---

**11. What to Actually Invest In (Leadership Perspective)**
Rather than developing "taste" in their PMs, what should product leaders actually build? Analyze:
- **Discovery skills** — how leaders ensure teams stay close to real customer needs
- **Human-to-human interaction** — the skills AI cannot replicate, and how leaders model and protect them
- **AI collaboration** — how leaders build AI-native working habits into their PM org
- **Evidence-based judgment** — moving from "I think" to "the data shows" as a cultural norm

Central question: *"It's not about your taste. It's about your customer's taste."* — How does this principle reshape how product leaders hire, coach, and evaluate their PMs?
