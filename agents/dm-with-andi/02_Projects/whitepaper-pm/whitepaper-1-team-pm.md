## Perplexity-Prompt (aktuell)

---

**Research prompt: The Future of the Product Manager in the Product Operating Model**

I am writing a white paper on how the role of the Product Manager is evolving within the context of the Product Operating Model (POM). The ideas below are my initial hypotheses — please challenge them where evidence or practice contradicts them.

---

**Core Beliefs (framing for the entire paper)**
- The Product Operating Model makes more sense than ever. As AI accelerates execution, the need for clear ownership, empowered teams, and outcome-driven structure intensifies — not diminishes.
- Outcome over output. The shift from shipping features to delivering measurable value is the defining discipline of modern product work.
- Build to learn, not to earn. Prototyping and product discovery are a fundamental mindset shift: the primary purpose of building is to learn faster.
- Product sense is a competitive advantage. In a world where execution is increasingly commoditized by AI, judgment — knowing what to build, for whom, and why — becomes the scarce and differentiating skill.
- Agency is a learnable discipline. PMs who act without waiting for permission, clarity, or perfect information create disproportionate value.
- Learning by doing beats learning by reading. The 24/7 AI coach enables a new kind of fast, practice-based, self-directed learning.
- Strong leadership ≠ command-and-control. Real leadership — and good PM work — is about setting direction and guardrails, not dictating every decision. Decisions should sit with the person closest to the problem.
- The "flotilla of kayaks" model: teams move in an aligned direction while retaining independent exploration. Consultative decision-making — one person decides, incorporating input from relevant stakeholders — is the operating mode of empowered teams.

---

**1. Core thesis to explore**
As AI and leaner org structures reshape product teams, the PM role is bifurcating. Analyze the following scenarios — and challenge them:

- **Scenario A – The Explainer PM:** The PM becomes primarily a strategic translator — explaining vision, goals, and context to engineers who are increasingly autonomous.
- **Scenario B – The Strategic PM:** The PM absorbs responsibilities traditionally owned by the Product Director or GPM. Smaller teams, wider scope, more P&L ownership.
- **Scenario C – The Builder PM:** The PM builds hands-on prototypes and demos to validate faster. Vibe coding and low-code tools become core skills.
- **Scenario D – The Customer PM:** The PM spends significantly more time in direct customer contact.
- **Scenario E – The Growth PM:** The PM owns a full growth loop — acquisition, activation, retention, monetization — accountable for business metrics.

For each scenario: What triggers it? What does the PM's day look like? What skills are required? Are these scenarios mutually exclusive or concurrent?

---

**2. The Trio in Flux — What happens when roles disappear?**
The classic product trio is PM + UX + Engineering. Analyze each structural variant:

- **Trio intact** — PM + UX + Engineering in an AI-augmented world
- **No PM** — Engineering + UX without a dedicated PM
- **No UX** — PM + Engineering without a dedicated UX designer
- **Neither PM nor UX** — Engineering-led product, fully autonomous

For each: What type of product or company does it suit? What are the failure modes?

The trio is not just shrinking — it is fusing. Analyze the emergence of the **"Product Builder"**: a person with a shared foundation across disciplines (PM, design, engineering) and deep expertise in one area. This is not a generalist ideal — it is a new baseline all roles are expected to share. Key questions:
- AI raises the baseline: the repetitive, pattern-based ~80% of product work can now be done by anyone with the right tools. What remains are complex trade-offs requiring real judgment. How does this reshape role boundaries?
- Smaller, cross-functional teams: are they the natural outcome? What are the failure modes?
- Deep expertise remains critical for complex decisions — how do you preserve depth in a world pushing breadth?
- Ignoring AI tools is no longer optional — what does this mean for hiring and performance expectations?
- Leaders must create guardrails: security, QA, and review systems for AI-assisted workflows — how?

**3. What PMs must learn — skill implications**
- **Domain expertise** — technical, industry, business model
- **Judgment and product sense** — the scarce differentiating skill
- **Agency** — acting without waiting for permission, clarity, or perfect information; proactively driving decisions and momentum
- **Stakeholder management** — influence without authority
- **Communication and storytelling**
- **Data savviness & data drivenness** — collecting and evaluating all relevant data becomes dramatically easier with internally vibe-coded tools; PMs must learn to commission or build these tools and use data as the primary basis for decisions
- **Collaboration** — see section 7
- **AI-native skills** — see section 5

**5. AI-native skills for PMs**

- Prototyping with AI tools (v0, Cursor, etc.) — build to learn, not to earn
- Evaluating AI-generated outputs critically
- Using AI for customer research synthesis, competitive analysis, document creation

---

**6. The Role of the PRD in the Product vs. Project Model**
The PRD plays fundamentally different roles depending on model:

- **Product Model:** Prototype as spec. The PRD supplements discovery — enumerating use cases and non-functional requirements not captured in a prototype. The PRD must never replace discovery.
- **Project Model:** PRD written in lieu of discovery. The PM assumes they know what is "required" — one of the most common root causes of product failure.

Questions to explore: How are leading orgs using the PRD differently today? How does AI change this — does it make specless development more feasible, or increase the risk of skipping discovery?

---

**7. Collaboration as a rising imperative**
As teams get smaller and AI handles more execution:
- How does the PM become connective tissue between engineering, design, data, marketing, and leadership?
- What does healthy collaboration look like when engineers are more autonomous and AI agents participate in workflows?
- **Curiosity as a core skill** — genuinely wanting to understand *why* someone demands something builds trust and leads to better solutions than arguing about *what*
- **"Yes, and" from improv theater** — instead of blocking ideas, developing them further makes team conversations more productive and psychologically safer
- **Investing in relationships = moving faster** — teams that do relationship work move faster long-term, not slower; challenge the assumption that relationship-building is overhead
- **The human moat:** AI can generate ideas and write code — but navigating competing priorities, aligning stakeholders, and creating shared understanding remains genuine human work. Explore: which AI tools actively support stakeholder alignment, and how should PMs use them?

**8. The 24/7 AI Product Coach & The New Learning Imperative**
- How can AI tools serve as always-available product coaches — giving feedback on decisions, challenging assumptions, simulating customer interviews?
- The skill of learning extremely fast — often without relying on people, but with AI as a sparring partner
- Learning by doing beats learning by reading — how should PM development programs shift to practice-based, AI-assisted learning?

**11. What to Actually Invest In**
Rather than chasing "taste," what should PMs actually develop? Analyze and challenge:
- **Discovery skills** — understanding customers and matching solutions to real needs
- **Human-to-human interaction** — skills AI cannot replicate
- **AI collaboration** — learning to work with AI effectively as a thinking partner
- **Evidence-based judgment** — "taste without evidence is just opinion"
